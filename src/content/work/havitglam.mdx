## Overview

Havitglam is a Korean-market lifestyle app built for **1zLabs** (Seoul) — users build daily habits and self-care routines (vitamins, gym, yoga, water, reading), then verify they actually did them with a quick photo. They can share with "Glammers" and join 7-day challenges. One of three apps I shipped for 1zLabs while at Sadguru Soft.

## Problem &amp; context

Habit-tracker apps are everywhere. What made Havitglam different was the photo-verification loop — proof, not self-report — plus a social layer that turns "did I drink enough water today" into a shared activity.

That combination has product consequences. The app has to be photo-heavy on the storage side, fast on the gallery scroll, reliable on push delivery, and tuned for a Korean-language audience with its own design register.

## My approach &amp; key decisions

- **Photo verification flow with timestamp metadata.** Captured in-app, sent through a cached image pipeline before backing up — the proof artefact is fast to capture and durable to keep.
- **Optimised local image caching + cloud storage** to keep 60 fps performance under heavy photo rendering and gallery scrolling.
- **FCM** for the social-challenge push notifications and the daily-habit reminders.
- **Animations woven through the routine + challenge surfaces** so daily check-ins feel rewarding rather than rote.

## Architecture &amp; stack

- **Client:** Flutter (Dart) for Android.
- **Backend:** Firebase backing the routines, social graph and challenge state.
- **Notifications:** FCM for daily reminders and social-challenge events.
- **Media:** Camera capture + local cache + cloud storage pipeline.
- **Distribution:** Live on Google Play at `com.onezlabs.havitglam` (publisher: onezlabs / 1zLabs).

## Challenges

- **Photo-heavy 60 fps performance.** Gallery scrolling on phones with mid-tier hardware while photos are decoding is a classic stutter trap; image caching had to be aggressive but bounded.
- **Push reliability on Android in Korea.** FCM behaviour varies by OEM aggressive battery-saver behaviour — push delivery has to be tested on real devices, not just emulators.
- **Cultural fit.** A Korean lifestyle app speaks a different visual language than a Western fitness app; design + product decisions had to respect that.
